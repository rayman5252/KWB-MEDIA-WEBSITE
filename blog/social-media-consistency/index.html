Stop Posting Like It's a New Year's Resolution

By Ray Wolters, KWB Media · October 1, 2026

Every business owner I talk to has the same social media story. It goes like this: January, big plans. Post every day! Content calendar! Color-coded spreadsheet! By March the calendar is a rumor, and the last post on your page is a photo of a "Happy Memorial Day" graphic. In August.

I'm not judging. I make videos for a living and I've abandoned my own posting schedule more times than I've abandoned a diet. But after years of watching what works, here's the truth: the businesses winning on social aren't the most creative. They're the ones who showed up again next Tuesday.

What the numbers say

Buffer analyzed more than 52 million posts for its 2026 posting-frequency guide. Its baseline for most small businesses: 3 to 5 posts per week.

Notice what that number is not. It's not "twice a day." It's not "go viral or go home." Three to five a week is a Monday, a Wednesday and a Friday. That's a pace a human being with a day job can survive.

And it's a lot more useful than a heroic month followed by silence. Think about it from the customer's side. They land on your page, see a post from eleven weeks ago, and quietly wonder if you're still in business. Nobody wants to hire the guy whose last sign of life was a Halloween photo.

Why we all fall off the wagon

It's almost never laziness. It's that posting feels like a second job nobody hired you for. Here's what I see over and over:

  • No plan. Every post starts with "what the heck do I say today?" That's a terrible way to start anything.
  • Perfectionism. You wait for the perfect photo. The perfect photo never comes. Meanwhile your competitor posted a slightly blurry shot of a finished job and got three calls.
  • Doing it all yourself. You're running the business, answering the phones and now you're also the social media department. Congratulations on your promotion. There's no raise.

How to actually stay consistent

1. Pick a pace you can keep

Three posts a week you actually publish beats seven you plan to publish. Start small. You can always add more once the habit sticks. It's the same reason I don't start a workout plan by running a marathon. (I tried. The couch won.)

2. Batch it

Take an hour once a week, or a few hours once a month, and make a pile of content at the same time. Shoot several short videos in one sitting. Same shirt, same spot, different topics. Nobody will notice, and if they do, tell them it's a signature look.

3. Steal from your own day

You already have content: a job finished, a question a customer asks every week, a before and after, a quick tip. Answer one real question per post and you'll never run out of ideas. People don't need polish. They need to see that you know your stuff and that you're still around.

4. Mix it up

Short video, a photo, a graphic, a customer win. Variety keeps your page from looking like a copy machine got stuck. And yes, video earns its spot in that mix. If you want the numbers on that, I covered them in Video Marketing Statistics for 2026.

5. Hand it off when it stops being fun

If the posting keeps dying on your to-do list, that's not a character flaw. That's a sign it belongs on someone else's list. That's exactly why social media management exists.

The boring secret

A mediocre post on a regular schedule beats a brilliant one that shows up twice a year. Social media is less like a fireworks show and more like brushing your teeth. Nobody is impressed on any given day, but skip it for six months and people notice.
