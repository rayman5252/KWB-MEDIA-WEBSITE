Video Marketing Statistics for 2026: What the Numbers Mean for Your Business

By Ray Wolters, KWB Media · September 25, 2026

Every year somebody asks me whether video is "still worth it." Every year I say yes, and every year the numbers back me up, which is great, because I'd much rather be out shooting than arguing. So here are the 2026 statistics that matter, pulled from two of the biggest annual surveys in marketing, plus what I think they mean for a business owner in Hartford, Springfield or Boston. I read the reports so you don't have to. You're welcome.

Almost everybody is using video now

91% of businesses use video as a marketing tool, and 93% of video marketers call it an important part of their strategy. (Wyzowl, 2026)

Ninety-one percent. That's more businesses than can agree on where to get the best pizza in New Haven. Video isn't the "extra" anymore. It's table stakes. If your competitors have a video on their homepage and you don't, the customer notices, even if they couldn't tell you why. They just feel like the other guy has his act together.

It pays for itself

  • 82% of marketers say video has given them a good return on investment.
  • 85% say video has helped them generate leads.
  • 83% say video has directly increased sales.
  • 82% say video keeps visitors on their website longer.

That last one is sneaky important. The longer someone hangs around your site, the more likely they are to pick up the phone, and the more Google figures your page is worth showing to the next person. Think of it as the opposite of a first date where they check their watch at the appetizers.

Your customers want it

85% of people say a video has convinced them to buy a product or service, and 96% have watched an explainer video to learn more about something they were considering. (Wyzowl, 2026)

And when people want to learn about a product, 63% say a short video is how they'd most like to do it. Not a PDF. Not a wall of text. Nobody has ever said, "Wow, I really wish this brochure had more paragraphs."

Short-form is winning

In HubSpot's 2026 State of Marketing report, a survey of more than 1,500 marketers, short-form video ranked #1 for return on investment, ahead of long-form video and live streaming. Think 15 to 90 seconds: a quick product demo, a customer shout-out, a behind-the-scenes clip for Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts or LinkedIn. Roughly the attention span of a golden retriever at a tennis ball factory.

The good news: one well-planned shoot day can produce a hero video and a stack of short clips cut from the same footage. Work hard once, post all month. That's how we plan most projects now.

AI is in the edit bay (but people still want people)

63% of video marketers say they've used AI tools to help create or edit videos. We use them too, for things like transcripts, captions and rough cuts. The robots are great at the boring stuff. But HubSpot found that 62.7% of marketers believe we need more unique, human-centered content to stand out from all the AI-generated stuff. Real faces, real places and real stories are the differentiator. AI can do a lot of things, but it still can't stand on a frozen Connecticut lake in February and pretend it's not cold.

What this means for your business

  1. Start with one strong video. A 60 to 90 second brand or explainer video on your homepage does a lot of heavy lifting, and it never calls in sick.
  2. Cut it down. Turn that shoot into 5 to 10 short clips for social media. Same footage, way more mileage.
  3. Show your people. Your team and your customers are the one thing AI can't fake convincingly. Yes, even Dave from accounting. Especially Dave from accounting.
  4. Put it where people look. Your website, Google Business Profile, YouTube and LinkedIn. A great video nobody sees is just an expensive screensaver.
